FabLab Spinderihallerne

About FabLab

FabLab is a creative, high-tech prototype workshop in Spinderihallerne, where citizens, students, companies and entrepreneurs can work with digital production technologies and develop ideas into concrete prototypes.

What is a FabLab?

A FabLab (Fabrication Laboratory) is an open, digital workshop where ideas can be turned into physical prototypes using technologies such as 3D printers, laser cutters, CNC and electronics tools. The concept originates from MIT's Center for Bits and Atoms in the early 2000s, where the goal was to give more people access to advanced production technology and a hands-on way of learning through experimentation.

FabLabs around the world are built on a common Fab Lab Charter. The Charter describes, among other things, that a FabLab should be a place for learning, innovation and knowledge sharing, that users should have the opportunity for open access to some extent, and that methods, experiences and projects are shared with others. At the same time the Charter emphasises responsible use of machines, safety in the workshop and respect for people, materials and ideas.

Who can use it?

FabLab Spinderihallerne is for everyone, but with different offerings throughout the week. On Tuesdays 15:00–20:00 it is publicly open, where hobby users, educational environments, businesses and entrepreneurs can get help from instructors and work on both small and large prototype projects. The other days are primarily used for digital fabrication workshops: Wednesday is typically booked by SDU for teaching sessions with instructors in machine use, basic electronics, soldering and PCB manufacturing, while Thursday accommodates further education of teachers, consultants and educators through Make Academy as well as guidance and sparring for companies on, amongst other things, prototype manufacturing. The remaining days host workshops for other user groups.
